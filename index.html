2026.08 — Geopolitics

The Tuff Stone Grid

Walking through urban grids shaped by pink volcanic tuff reveals a stark duality: the visual permanence of ancient stone contrasted with the rapid fluidity of modern software systems.

Under Soviet-era planning, engineering and strategic calculation were not merely professional choices but cultural survival mechanisms. Chess was embedded into early education, shaping how technical logic, strategic depth, and modular design evolved across generations.

On the Black Sea Coast

The coastline in early summer retains a quiet, muted grey. Sitting near the water with a deck of historic cards, teaching rules preserved across centuries offers a sense of temporal continuity.

Exile is rarely a sudden rupture; it is an accumulation of landscapes, dialects, and small rituals repeated in new settings.

2026.08

"Digital nomadism" does not exist.

"Digital nomadism" does not exist.

It is a complete fraud of language. Most are neither nomads nor guests.

A guest honors the host and acknowledges the weight of a place.

The modern remote worker treats sovereign nations as a service provider: cheap coffee, discounted housing, and surface-level exchanges with other transients, checking off locations from a catalog and running the second friction appears. Most never stay long enough to understand local responsibility.

Historical nomadism was the exact opposite.

Real nomads were bound by seasonal cycles, fiercely tied to a collective, and driven by geography. Mobility was how a group endured hardship together, not how an individual evaded responsibility.

What gets marketed as freedom is just hyper-individualism in liquid form: sliding across surfaces without ever taking root, learning the language, or bearing weight.

Real autonomy requires verticality.

To be vertical is to reject a flattened world. It means choosing to anchor into actual soil, accept local constraints, build structures that outlast a season, and bond with locals to share in their reality, both the good and the bad.

Horizontal mobility is cheap and weightless. Verticality demands commitment.

This is the fundamental boundary: one remains a customer browsing a menu of host nations without caring. The other becomes an operator who invests in the place they inhabit.

"Digital nomad" is a term invented by a naive, horizontal society. Real societies need vertical builders, not surface-level buyers.

Stop mistaking displacement for independence.

One consumes the landscape. The other becomes a pillar.
