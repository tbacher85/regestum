2026.08 — Geopolitics & Strategy

Armenia and Digital Feudalism

Armenia will not build its future by consuming software built elsewhere.

While the local tech sector continues to imitate Western models and Western messianism, it ignores what the digital landscape has actually become: a neo-feudal system.

Look at the warning before us: Europe itself is already a digital vassal. Having traded material sovereignty for consumer convenience, an entire continent now pays perpetual tribute to foreign clouds, foreign platforms, and foreign chips. It regulates tools it no longer owns.

For a landlocked country facing existential geopolitical realities, following this path is an uncompromising mistake—it is a slow surrender.

A handful of private monopolies have replaced state authority, dictated terms, and seized control of global data flows. Nations that do not own their tools are no longer sovereign actors. They are tributaries tied to virtual fiefdoms.

What past generations built on this soil with stone fortresses, engineers must rebuild today with modern code, servers, and physical infrastructure.

True resilience is not software integration. It is material sovereignty:

  • Owning physical foundations: data centers and networks, so no foreign giant holds the key to national continuity.
  • Cultivating algorithmic autonomy: building indigenous IP rather than serving as cheap outsourced labor for overseas platforms.
  • Engineering digital fortresses: encryption and communication systems built for real conflict, not idealized free markets.

Building a tech ecosystem in Armenia is not about seeking foreign validation, nor trading one dependence for another.

Code, silicon, and data are not consumer goods.

They are the granite of national permanence.

2026.06 — History & Posture

Tiridates I on the Via Sacra

In 66 AD, King Tiridates I walked these exact basalt stones to meet Nero.

He didn't take a ship across the Mediterranean. To respect ancient rituals of land and water, he chose a 9-month overland march across Europe, riding horseback and walking beside 3,000 Armenian nobles.

Think about that level of intentional friction: 9 months of dust, logistics, and total discipline just to step onto the Via Sacra and reach the negotiating table on his own terms.

The result? Nero was so captivated by this unyielding posture that he didn't just confirm the crown—he poured millions into rebuilding Artaxata and sent Roman master builders to erect the Temple of Garni, which was reassembled stone by stone and stands in Armenia to this day.

Modern culture is addicted to cheap shortcuts, friction-free outreach, and instant validation. We want the capital without the journey. We want leverage without walking the road.

Tiridates understood an unforgiving law of posture:

The weight of your presence at the table is directly proportional to the discipline of your path.

True strategic sovereignty is never handed to you. It is forged in every single obstacle you choose to face.

2026.05 — Essay

"Digital nomadism" does not exist.

"Digital nomadism" does not exist. You have all heard about the frictionless remote worker operating from anywhere. It is a complete fraud of language. Most are neither nomads nor guests.

A guest honors the host and acknowledges the weight of a place.

The modern remote worker treats sovereign nations as a service provider: cheap coffee, discounted housing, and surface-level exchanges with other transients, checking off locations from a catalog and running the second friction appears. Most never stay long enough to understand local responsibility.

Historical nomadism was the exact opposite.

Real nomads were bound by seasonal cycles, fiercely tied to a collective, and driven by geography. Mobility was how a group endured hardship together, not how an individual evaded responsibility.

What gets marketed as freedom is just hyper-individualism in liquid form: sliding across surfaces without ever taking root, learning the language, or bearing weight.

Real autonomy requires verticality.

To be vertical is to reject a flattened world. It means choosing to anchor into actual soil, accept local constraints, build structures that outlast a season, and bond with locals to share in their reality, both the good and the bad.

Horizontal mobility is cheap and weightless. Verticality demands commitment.

This is the fundamental boundary: one remains a customer browsing a menu of host nations without caring. The other becomes an operator who invests in the place they inhabit.

"Digital nomad" is a term invented by a naive, horizontal society. Real societies need vertical builders, not surface-level buyers.

Stop mistaking displacement for independence.

One consumes the landscape. The other becomes a pillar.
